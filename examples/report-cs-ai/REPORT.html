Executive Summary

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a language model on its own rollouts while a teacher, the same model additionally given privileged information (PI) such as a verified solution, scores each token; the student sees only the question [1]. The sources read (all 2026 preprints, none peer-review-checked here) agree on the mechanism and on the benefit claimed: a dense per-token signal, reported in the founding paper to match GRPO with significantly improved token efficiency, without a stated multiplier [1]. They also agree, with differing strength, that the asymmetry is a source of bias. A rich-context teacher suppresses the expression of uncertainty, shortens responses and degraded math performance by up to 40 % in three models [2]; PI inflates teacher confidence on solution-implied tokens and deflates it on deliberation tokens such as "Wait" [4]; raw oracle solutions are linked to answer leakage and late-stage collapse [5]. Loss of output diversity under self-distillation is measured directly with falling rollout diversity and flattening pass@k, but on graph path-finding and science QA, not mathematics [3]. For mathematics the evidence on diversity is indirect.

Three levers are studied. Token weighting: position-weighted OPSD gains +1.0 and +1.1 Avg@12 on AIME 2024/2025 [6], while entropy-triggered gating [4] and entropy-aware forward/reverse KL mixing [7] propose different reliability criteria. Nature of PI: the correct reference gave no consistent benefit and a solution from another problem sometimes beat it [12][13], whereas trajectory-grounded guidance beat the full solution [14]. Teacher dynamics: full exposure is not reliably best, mismatch grows with exposure, and a learned reveal ratio adds +0.95 to +2.33 Avg@12 over OPSD [15]; temporal annealing appears in [5]. No source seen compares frozen and EMA teachers.

Established: the training recipe works at 1.7B-8B scale and the teacher's behaviour is context-induced. Disputed: whether the PI content matters, which tokens deserve weight, and whether diversity collapse in math is caused by the asymmetry or by the reverse-KL objective. The report rests on 18 sources, mostly single-team abstracts; see Limitations.

Introduction

Question: how can on-policy self-distillation, with a teacher that is the model itself conditioned on information unavailable at test time, be critically analysed; how does the asymmetry give both a learning signal and a bias, in particular a progressive collapse of the diversity of reasoning paths; how do token weighting, the nature of the PI and teacher dynamics with guidance decay govern that symptom; what is established and what disputed in mathematical reasoning. Main Analysis

Finding 1 — The privileged teacher gives a dense, cheap per-token signal, but it is the model's conditional distribution, not ground truth

The founding formulation has the teacher policy conditioning on privileged information, for instance verified reasoning traces, while the student sees only the question; training minimises a per-token divergence on the student's own rollouts, and the authors report that it matches GRPO with significantly improved token efficiency on competition-level mathematical reasoning benchmarks [1]; the paper gives no single multiplier, only the training configurations (one rollout of 1024 sampled tokens per problem against eight rollouts of 16k tokens for GRPO) [1]. This efficiency claim comes from a single team. Variants keep the same skeleton: a policy-gradient hybrid combines group-relative verifier advantages, full-vocabulary student-to-teacher reverse KL and a reference-policy KL, and reports better stability and performance than RLVR and self-distillation baselines, without naming models or data in its abstract [9]; another shows that vanilla OPSD is the β=1 member of a family that interpolates geometrically between the reference policy and the privileged teacher, with return-to-go credit, outperforming vanilla OPSD on mathematical benchmarks [8]; a hybrid applies privileged rollouts, with ground-truth information, to prompts where all rollouts fail, on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct [10]. The learning signal is therefore twofold: the verifier reward (when present) and the teacher's token distribution, which is denser than a single outcome bit.

What qualifies it. The teacher is not an oracle: its token preferences are produced by the same network under a different context, and the sources show that this context shapes behaviour. One analysis states that OPSD gains do not necessarily come from access to the reference solution and that the teacher's context-induced behaviour is an important factor [13]. Another reports that the rich-context teacher suppresses uncertainty expression [2]. The reverse KL used as divergence is mode-seeking and, in an external-teacher setting, is reported to reduce generation diversity, which motivated adding forward KL when teacher entropy is high [7]; that paper does not use a privileged self-teacher, so it is a mechanism analogy, not direct evidence. Also, efficiency claims are not shown here to hold against equally tuned baselines; the abstracts read do not report seeds or error bars, so I cannot state protocol-level dispersion.

Consequence: the asymmetry creates the signal (the teacher knows where the solution goes) and the bias (it knows too much, so it penalises exploration the student legitimately needs). Verdict: established that the recipe trains and is reported token-efficient in the authors' settings (one team); open whether the efficiency survives independent replication.

Finding 2 — The asymmetry biases the student toward over-confident, shorter, less exploratory reasoning; direct evidence of diversity collapse exists, but outside mathematics

Evidence on the bias. In mathematical reasoning with Qwen3-8B, DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-7B and Olmo3-7B-Instruct, self-distillation reduced response length while degrading performance, with drops of up to 40 %, which the authors attribute to suppression of epistemic verbalization: conditioning the teacher on rich information suppresses uncertainty expression [2]. A mutual-information analysis finds that PI inflates the teacher's confidence on tokens already implied by the solution and deflates it on deliberation tokens (Wait, Let, Maybe); its fix is gated by an entropy trigger that switches the term off once teacher entropy collapses, across five models from 4B to 30B, with up to 11.5 points over GRPO and 2 to 10x fewer training steps [4]. Raw oracle solutions are said to induce over-conditioned teacher distributions, implicit answer leakage and late-stage training collapse [5]. A further paper observes that on successful rollouts the teacher mechanism overwrites the student's own choices and suppresses its own reasoning, and proposes to reverse the signal on correct rollouts [11].

Evidence on diversity. The only direct measurement found is for on-policy self-distillation with sampled demonstrations, where rollout diversity decreases and pass@k curves flatten; the mechanism proposed is that self-distillation amplifies existing probability gaps and concentrates mass on already-dominant modes [3]. Its tasks are graph path-finding and science QA, not mathematics. In mathematics, the closest numbers are indirect: the hybrid that applies privileged self-distillation only to all-fail prompts reports pass@4 +0.8 to +1.1 % and pass@8 +0.4 to +1.7 % while keeping greedy accuracy [10], which shows the distillation weight can be tuned to protect exploration but is not a measurement of collapse. RLVR comparators show the same phenomenon without any teacher: Pass@1 improves while high-k Pass@k degrades under overtraining, and restricting updates to zero-success problems lifts Pass@256 above the base model [16]. So diversity loss is a general property of outcome-driven training, and the question of what the asymmetry adds on top is open.

Consequence: the progressive collapse is best described as a hypothesis with converging indirect support in math (confidence inflation, shortened reasoning, answer leakage, late-stage collapse) and direct support in two non-math tasks. Verdict: diversity collapse in mathematical OPSD, open (no direct pass@k-versus-training-step measurement on math seen).

Finding 3 — Lever one, token weighting: all sources agree uniform weighting is wrong, none agrees on which tokens to trust

Position-weighted OPSD observes that standard OPSD weights all generated tokens equally and shows that a position score predicts teacher-token reliability with AUROC 0.83, whereas local uncertainty scores reach at most 0.57 (Qwen3-4B setting); weighting by increasing position with a clipped forward-KL target gives AIME 2024 and 2025 Avg@12 gains of +1.0 and +1.1 [6]. A different diagnosis comes from the mutual-information paper: the problem is deliberation tokens being deflated, handled by a PMI-based term that an entropy gate disables once teacher entropy collapses [4]. A third scheme modulates token-level advantages with temporal annealing inside a meta-reflective self-distillation [5]. The β-OPSD family uses return-to-go credit assignment [8]. In the external-teacher setting, entropy-aware distillation adds forward KL when teacher entropy is high and reports Pass@8 gains of +1.37, +2.39 and +5.05 on Qwen3-0.6B, 1.7B and 4B-Base across six math benchmarks [7]. A neighbouring contrastive method assigns per-token credit by contrasting predictions under positive and negative prompts, but is not a privileged self-distillation method and also covers text-to-image [17].

Qualification. The criteria are incompatible in principle: position-based [6] and entropy- or confidence-based [4][7] rules select different tokens, and the position paper itself mentions benign solution diversity as an ambiguity of entropy-based reliability. Gains reported are small (about one point) and come from single papers with abstract-level evidence. One title (read as title only) states that privileged likelihood is not automatically value and proposes three checks for token credit in OPSD [18]; I could not read its content, so it is only a signal that the token-credit question is contested.

Consequence: token weighting plausibly governs the collapse symptom because it decides whether the confident, solution-implied tokens or the exploratory deliberation tokens dominate the gradient, but no source ties a weighting scheme to measured diversity (pass@k) in math. Verdict: that weighting matters, probable; which weighting is right, open.

Finding 4 — Lever two, the nature of the privileged information: more or "more correct" PI is not better, and the teacher's context-induced behaviour explains much of the gain

Three mathematics studies at 1.7B-8B scale converge in an unexpected direction. Replacing the paired reference with a problem and solution from a different example (OP2SD) improves over the base model across three models and three math benchmarks and remains competitive with OPSD [13]. The correct reference does not provide a consistent benefit, and a solution from another problem can outperform the correct solution on several mathematical benchmarks (Qwen3 1.7B-8B) [12]. Replacing the full solution by trajectory-grounded guidance, describing initial state, goal conditions, constraints and a selected state-transition path, gives the highest aggregate question-only accuracy among compared methods, and guidance relevance and path coherence contribute [14]. Conversely, the richest context is the most harmful: rich-context teachers suppress uncertainty [2], and raw oracle solutions cause leakage and late collapse [5]. Exposure is also a PI variable: teacher-student mismatch grows monotonically as the teacher sees more privileged reasoning [15].

Qualification. [12] and [13] are two papers released close together, probably independent but both critiques of OPSD; [14] is a method paper from a third group. Their abstracts do not give seeds or dispersion, so differences between "competitive" and "better" cannot be judged. The interpretation that follows is mine: if an unrelated solution works almost as well, the signal that the student learns is partly a style or format prior (confident, concise, solution-like), which would also explain the loss of deliberation behaviours seen in [2] and [4].

Consequence: PI should be seen as a control on teacher behaviour rather than as information transfer. Verdict: that the correct full solution is not necessary and not reliably best, probable (three sources, math, small to mid models); what PI is optimal (structure, hints, partial), open.

Finding 5 — Lever three, teacher dynamics and guidance decay: schedules help in the few cases tested, but frozen versus EMA teachers and explicit decay laws are essentially unstudied

The adaptive-exposure paper states that full exposure is not reliably the best choice, that mismatch grows monotonically with exposure, and a learnable reveal ratio yields +0.95, +2.05 and +2.33 Avg@12 over OPSD on AIME 24, AIME 25 and HMMT 25 for Qwen3-1.7B, 4B and 8B [15]. Temporal annealing is part of a second method, which also names late-stage training collapse as the failure it addresses [5]. Gating by teacher entropy [4] and the interpolation coefficient β between reference and teacher [8] are further dials that change over a run or across configurations.

Qualification. Two targeted searches for an EMA, frozen or stale teacher comparison, and for explicit hint annealing in LLM self-distillation, returned no relevant source. So the claim that the teacher "moves with the student" (the on-policy teacher shares the student's weights and thus drifts) is a structural fact of the setting [1], but its consequences for collapse are not isolated experimentally in anything I read. Whether decaying guidance prevents or merely delays collapse is not demonstrated: the cited results are final-accuracy gains, not diversity curves.

Consequence: this lever is the least evidenced. Verdict: open.

Synthesis & Insights

The sources read converge on a coherent picture, which is my reading and not any single paper's claim. The asymmetry is productive because the teacher's context makes its token distribution informative on the solution path; it is biased because the same context makes the teacher over-confident on tokens the solution implies and under-weights deliberation. A student trained to match that distribution on its own rollouts inherits the confident, shorter style [2][4], and the reverse-KL, mode-seeking objective [7] plus on-policy sampling then narrows the set of paths the student visits [3]. The three levers act on three parts of this chain: token weighting on which tokens carry the bias [4][6], PI on how strong the teacher's confidence inflation is [12][13][14], and exposure schedules on how long the student is exposed to it [5][15].

Disagreements. (a) PI content: the critiques [12][13] suggest the correct solution is not what makes OPSD work, whereas [14] suggests relevant, coherent guidance helps; these are compatible if what matters is the teacher's induced behaviour, but no source tests that directly. (b) Token reliability: position [6] versus confidence or entropy [4][7]. (c) Diversity: measured where it is easiest to measure (non-math [3]) and inferred elsewhere. What is new relative to RLVR diversity work [16] is that the cause of narrowing is not only the reward but the teacher's conditional distribution. Second-order effect: because pass@1 improves while high-k pass@k can fall [16], reporting only Avg@12 or pass@1, as most abstracts here do [6][15], can hide the symptom under study.

Limitations & Caveats

Eighteen sources carry the report; all are 2026 preprints, nearly all read at abstract level only, so figures are as the authors state them and no table was re-read. Most claims rest on one or two teams, so the triangulation rule (three independent clusters) is met only for the point that the privileged context induces teacher behaviour and that the correct solution is not necessary ([2], [12], [13], arguably [14]); the diversity-collapse claim has one direct source outside math [3]. No source reported seeds or error bars in the abstracts, so gains of about one point [6] may be within noise. Empty angles: frozen versus EMA teacher; explicit hint-decay schedules; hint-guided RL and context distillation as neighbouring work (angle 8 returned nothing relevant). One title was seen but not read [18]; other candidates seen by title only (privileged hidden flow, adaptive-view and Bayesian self-distillation variants) are not cited. The search stopped at the call ceiling reserved for verification after two waves, the second of which still produced new eligible documents, so saturation was not demonstrated. OpenAlex and dblp contributed almost nothing, so non-arXiv literature may be under-covered. No claim is made on models above 30B or on benchmarks other than the competition-style math sets named.

Recommendations

For analysis of this family of methods: report pass@k (k up to 64 or more), response length, entropy and a reasoning-behaviour measure (uncertainty or deliberation tokens) alongside Avg@k, and across training steps; compare against a PI-from-another-problem control [13] and a no-PI self-distillation control; vary exposure with a fixed schedule versus an adaptive one [15]. To watch: independent replications of [12][13]; the content of [18]; any paper comparing frozen, EMA and live teachers. To search next: the sources behind angle 8 (hint-guided RL, context distillation) with other keywords; proceedings versions of these preprints; evaluation on non-Qwen families beyond [2].
